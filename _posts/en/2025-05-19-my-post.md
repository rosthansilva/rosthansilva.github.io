# Reflections on the Professional Trajectory in DevOps

## The Beginning: Specialization as a Foundation
<p align="center">
  <img src="https://cdn.hashnode.com/res/hashnode/image/upload/v1707247494948/4ce6b875-9bad-4e83-8815-4666d06f8a92.jpeg?auto=compress,format&format=webp" alt="Specialization" />
</p>

They say "it’s all roses at the beginning," but in the world of technology, that doesn't always apply. Starting out as a humble English teacher, I had the opportunity to join the Infnet Institute—a renowned institution in Rio de Janeiro. Back then, I had access to high-quality courses that paved my way to becoming a Linux Sysadmin.

For a long time, I focused all my efforts on mastering Linux, understanding that specialization was key to my development. However, I eventually realized that being a specialist in just one technology could limit my growth opportunities. So, my message to beginners is: **Choose a specialty to start, but keep your mind open to expanding your horizons in the future.**

## Mid-Career: Navigating Market Waters

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://www.pnoconsultants.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/market-analysis.jpg" alt="Market Analysis" />
</p>

By the middle of my career, I had mastered Linux, containers, Git, Kubernetes (the "ancient" kubeadm), Python, and a lot of networking. I felt the first waves of a new area called DevOps and shifted 100% of my focus to this more complex role. At that moment, I was making a strategic move: I didn't want to be just the Linux specialist automating deployments; I wanted to be the generalist the market was looking for—the DevOps Engineer. 

If it requires effort, **DO IT! Better late than "later"!** Invest in courses, seek out people with a strong market presence, and learn from those who truly know the field. Once you gain solid experience, you will naturally become a great Mid-level or Senior professional.

## The Career Plateau: Challenges and Renewal

<img src="https://preview.redd.it/just-senior-dev-things-v0-o81ooyu9tzy81.png?auto=webp&s=c3d73824660b1b20347130129fb35d96a6a1d922" alt="Senior Dev Life" />
</p>

Now for the "realest" and most valuable tip, specifically aimed at the more senior crowd.

### To the experienced folks: **Change companies!** 😊

Imagine a Senior who has been at the same company for 10 years. They switch jobs and suddenly realize they are performing at a Mid-level because their tech stack doesn't cover what is currently required for a Senior role. Scary, right?

I've often noticed that as a Junior, I could outperform many Seniors who gained all their experience at a single company. They became "aquarium fish" who forgot what the ocean looks like. So, if you are a Senior and you notice the market is asking for things you don't have, or that the "new kids" seem to know all the "sexiest" technologies... man, **let go of Java already.**

> "Just kidding 🐧, Java ♨️ is actually cool; it's the fan club that ruins it."

Start organizing yourself slowly. Learn new things and try to gain experience in other companies or side projects. I always say a Senior is a Senior, but sometimes the Senior has to *want* it too, right?

Another advantage of changing companies is **organizational knowledge**. As a professional, you need (or it would be a huge help) to know where you want to position yourself within an organization. You only get this feeling/insight by changing environments. Eventually, professional reinvention can be tiring. A high-performance culture is great, but running a motor without fresh oil will eventually break it!

From my perspective, it is essential for a high-level senior to have worked in several good companies. Even if this results in a Senior who is perhaps "less technical" and more **decisive**. Someone who truly knows what a company needs and does what is necessary to help the entire "living organism" of the business.

I see a lot of "weak" Seniors in the market—people who only know $X$ number of technologies and that’s it. Being a Senior or Specialist, in my view, is closer to **decision-making** than just knowing the latest tools. And how did I learn this? **By changing companies!**

Today it’s easier to be a Senior; there are many new technologies and areas to explore, but don’t let that take the driver's seat in your career. Don’t just be the "hard skills" person. Learn to communicate and say **"It depends"** with the frequency a true Senior should. Not every company accepts a "simple Senior."
